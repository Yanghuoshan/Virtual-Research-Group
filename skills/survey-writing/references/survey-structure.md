# Survey Structure

Procedures for the survey-writing skill only. They shape the manuscript; they never authorize new retrieval, citation verification, or global state writes.

## Scope Restatement

- Open with the review question, inclusion criteria, and period as recorded in the screening ledger.
- State the coverage limits in the introduction, not buried in a footnote: databases or corpora actually searched, languages, dates.

## Organization

- Prefer thematic or method-driven sections that map to evidence-table rows.
- Each section states what the grouped studies share, where they disagree, and under what conditions.
- Chronological narration only where the development itself is the claim, supported by dated rows.

## Citation Discipline

- Cite only entries from the verified bibliography with their recorded locators.
- A study is characterized by what its recorded entry supports; preprint and published versions are distinguished.
- Disagreements between studies are reported explicitly; no averaging across incompatible estimands.

## Tables

- Comparison tables copy semantics from the evidence tables; columns declare their meaning.
- Empty cells are marked "not reported", never filled from memory.

## Coverage and Limitations

- Report the ledger counts: identified, screened, included, excluded by reason.
- "We did not find" is stated as a coverage limit, never as evidence of absence.
- The final section lists what the survey cannot conclude and why.
